## 3. Score Contribution & Bottleneck Analysis

### Which component contributes least?
The **Semantic Score** contributes the least. Despite having a weight of 15% (`WEIGHT_SEMANTIC = 0.15`), the average semantic score is only **25.80%**, contributing an average of **3.87 points** to the final candidate score.

### Which component is suppressing scores most?
The **Semantic Score** is also the major suppressor of scores. While candidates achieve nearly perfect scores in experience (average ~98.6%) and skills (average 84.95%), their final scores are capped in the low-to-mid 80s due to the low semantic similarity value.

### Does semantic scoring remain the bottleneck?
**Yes.** The cosine similarity TF-IDF check is extremely strict and calibrated. Even for candidates with highly relevant profiles, the TF-IDF overlap rarely yields raw scores above 0.20 (which, after the 1.5 calibration multiplier, caps around 30.0% to 33.0%). Integrating deep-learning embeddings (such as Sentence-Transformers) instead of a simple TF-IDF vectorizer would resolve this bottleneck.

### Are role-fit multipliers over-aggressive?
**No.** All Top 20 candidates have a multiplier of `1.00`. However, the multipliers are critical for weeding out non-technical or non-specialized candidates. Without the role-fit multiplier (as seen in Scenario B), general software engineers creep into the Top 10, dilution of search/AI focus occurs, and the matching purity decreases.

### Are penalties triggering too frequently?
**No.** None of the Top 20 candidates triggered any of the three disqualifier penalties (consulting company penalty, job hopper penalty, or LangChain wrapper-only penalty). This shows the penalties are well-calibrated to target only specific undesirable profiles without penalizing high-quality, specialized talent.
